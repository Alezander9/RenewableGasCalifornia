# RNG Demand Saturation - California Transportation Fuel Market

The Renewable Natural Gas (RNG) market in California faces risks from the near-complete saturation of its primary demand niche—Natural Gas Vehicles (NGVs)—and long-term displacement by heavy-duty electric vehicles (EVs).

## Current NGV Market Saturation
As of 2024, RNG has nearly reached its maximum penetration within the California natural gas vehicle fuel market.
- **Market Share:** RNG accounts for **86%** of all on-road fuel used in NGVs in California [5](#ref-5).
- **Volume:** In 2024, this represented **666 million GGE** of RNG out of a total 774 million GGE of natural gas fuel used [5](#ref-5).
- **Dominance:** California is the largest consumer of RNG as transportation fuel in the U.S., representing **37.58%** of national use [5](#ref-5).

## Total Heavy-Duty Market Constraints
While RNG dominates the NGV niche, its ability to displace conventional fuels in the broader heavy-duty vehicle (HDV) market is limited by the small size of the NGV fleet relative to the total trucking sector.
- **Maximum Displacement:** Cost-viable RNG is projected to displace at most **8.9%** of California's total HDV fuel demand by 2030 [6](#ref-6).
- **In-State Production Limits:** Even if in-state production were maximized, it would likely only meet **19%** of total HDV demand, subject to significant economic and investment uncertainties [6](#ref-6).

## Regulatory and Policy Prioritization
State-level regulations, particularly California’s Advanced Clean Truck (ACT) rule and updated [Low Carbon Fuel Standard (LCFS)](../regulations/lcfs_program.md), increasingly prioritize Zero-Emission Vehicles (ZEVs) such as Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs) over Near-Zero Emission Vehicles (NZEVs) like RNG-powered trucks [1](#ref-1) [2](#ref-2). This shift compels Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) to focus their development and sales efforts on ZEV platforms, potentially reducing the availability and support for RNG vehicle options.

## Long-Term Policy-Driven Saturation
Regulatory updates are signaling an eventual end to RNG's growth incentives in the transportation sector.
- **LCFS Phase-out:** CARB has proposed phasing out "avoided methane" crediting for RNG projects starting in 2040 [7](#ref-7). This change is expected to significantly reduce the financial incentives for new RNG projects, signaling a strategic shift toward full electrification [7](#ref-7).

## Financial and Incentive Disadvantage
Public funding and purchase incentives are increasingly directed toward ZEV projects [3](#ref-3). Federal and state credits often favor BEVs, which creates a competitive hurdle for RNG fleets as the total cost of ownership (TCO) for BEVs continues to decrease [4](#ref-4).

## Technological Displacement and Adoption S-Curve
BEV technology is following a rapid improvement "S-curve" in terms of range and cost efficiency [4](#ref-4). While RNG has been considered a viable solution for the "messy middle" of decarbonization in regional and long-haul [transport sector](../industry_overview/transportation_market.md), the maturation of BEV technology may shorten this transition window, potentially stranding RNG infrastructure investments earlier than anticipated [4](#ref-4).

## Revenue Erosion (LCFS Credits)
Regulatory updates to programs like the LCFS are tightening the requirements for RNG credit generation. For instance, recent CARB updates limit ["book-and-claim"](../carbon_credit_markets/book_and_claim_accounting.md) accounting to gas injected into pipelines serving California [2](#ref-2). Additionally, potential future regulations on livestock methane could further alter the carbon intensity (CI) benefits and credit-generating potential of RNG [2](#ref-2).

## Market Segment Competition
BEVs, having established dominance in short-haul segments like terminal tractors and step vans, are now aggressively entering the regional haul Class 8 market [4](#ref-4). This encroachment targets segments that were previously considered strongholds for RNG due to its range and refueling capabilities.
