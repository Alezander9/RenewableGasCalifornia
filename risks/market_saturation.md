# Market Saturation (Transport) - Risk: Heavy-Duty Electric Vehicles

The Renewable Natural Gas (RNG) market faces significant long-term risks from the rapid advancement and regulatory prioritization of heavy-duty electric vehicles (EVs). While RNG has served as a transition fuel, several factors threaten its market share in the transport sector.

## Regulatory and Policy Prioritization
State-level regulations, particularly California’s Advanced Clean Truck (ACT) rule and updated Low Carbon Fuel Standard (LCFS), increasingly prioritize Zero-Emission Vehicles (ZEVs) such as Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs) over Near-Zero Emission Vehicles (NZEVs) like RNG-powered trucks [1](#ref-1) [2](#ref-2). This shift compels Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) to focus their development and sales efforts on ZEV platforms, potentially reducing the availability and support for RNG vehicle options.

## Financial and Incentive Disadvantage
Public funding and purchase incentives are increasingly directed toward ZEV projects [3](#ref-3). Federal and state credits often favor BEVs, which creates a competitive hurdle for RNG fleets as the total cost of ownership (TCO) for BEVs continues to decrease [4](#ref-4).

## Technological Displacement and Adoption S-Curve
BEV technology is following a rapid improvement "S-curve" in terms of range and cost efficiency [4](#ref-4). While RNG has been considered a viable solution for the "messy middle" of decarbonization in regional and long-haul transport, the maturation of BEV technology may shorten this transition window, potentially stranding RNG infrastructure investments earlier than anticipated [4](#ref-4).

## Revenue Erosion (LCFS Credits)
Regulatory updates to programs like the LCFS are tightening the requirements for RNG credit generation. For instance, recent CARB updates limit "book-and-claim" accounting to gas injected into pipelines serving California [2](#ref-2). Additionally, potential future regulations on livestock methane could further alter the carbon intensity (CI) benefits and credit-generating potential of RNG [2](#ref-2).

## Market Segment Competition
BEVs, having established dominance in short-haul segments like terminal tractors and step vans, are now aggressively entering the regional haul Class 8 market [4](#ref-4). This encroachment targets segments that were previously considered strongholds for RNG due to its range and refueling capabilities.
