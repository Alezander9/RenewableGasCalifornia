# SB 1383 Methane - Regulation

Senate Bill 1383 (SB 1383) is a California law aimed at reducing short-lived climate pollutants (SLCP), specifically methane, by diverting organic waste from landfills. [1](#ref-1) Organic waste in landfills emits methane, a potent greenhouse gas. [1](#ref-1)

## Organic Waste Diversion Goals
SB 1383 establishes the following targets for organic waste disposal reduction, based on 2014 levels:
* **50% reduction** by 2020 [1](#ref-1)
* **75% reduction** by 2025 [1](#ref-1)

## Edible Food Recovery Goal
The regulation also includes a goal to recover edible food that would otherwise be disposed of:
* **20% recovery** of currently disposed edible food for human consumption by 2025 [2](#ref-2)

## Impact on RNG Production
SB 1383 significantly impacts the Renewable Natural Gas (RNG) market through mandatory procurement requirements for jurisdictions. Cities and counties are required to procure recovered organic waste products, which includes biomethane (RNG) produced through anaerobic digestion. [3](#ref-3)

Common applications for RNG procured under SB 1383 include:
* **Transportation Fuel:** Powering refuse fleets and buses. [3](#ref-3)
* **Energy Generation:** Electricity and heating for buildings and schools. [3](#ref-3)

## Industry Impact: Waste Haulers

The implementation of SB 1383 in California has introduced both significant challenges and new opportunities for the waste management industry.

### Challenges for Waste Haulers

*   **Increased Operational Costs:** Waste haulers face higher transportation expenses, estimated at approximately $60 per ton, to transport organic waste to specialized transfer stations and composting facilities [4](#ref-4).
*   **Contamination Management:** Maintaining low contamination rates (typically between 5% and 15%) in organic waste streams remains a universal struggle, necessitating continuous monitoring and enforcement [4](#ref-4).
*   **Logistical Adjustments:** Compliance requires extensive changes to delivery routes, schedules, and the deployment of new specialized containers, such as the 130,000 bins deployed in Thousand Oaks [6](#ref-6).
*   **Reporting and Standardization:** There is a lack of standardized reporting forms across municipalities. Additionally, current software often lacks the automation required for efficient waste diversion reporting [5](#ref-5).
*   **Customer Engagement:** Reaching and engaging the multifamily and commercial sectors has proven difficult, requiring more intensive outreach efforts compared to residential sectors [4](#ref-4).

### Opportunities for Waste Haulers

*   **Infrastructure Investment:** Haulers that invest in owning and operating their own processing facilities, such as Athens Services' American Organics, can better control waste streams and ensure compliance with state diversion mandates [6](#ref-6).
*   **Long-term Contracts:** The complexity of SB 1383 compliance can lead to more stable, long-term municipal contracts, sometimes spanning 15 years, awarded through competitive bidding processes [6](#ref-6).
*   **Value-Added Services:** Companies can differentiate themselves by offering robust consumer education programs—including workshops and social media outreach—as well as custom automated reporting systems for their clients [4](#ref-4), [5](#ref-5).
*   **Community Partnerships:** Collaboration with local nonprofits for food recovery or community initiatives like donating compost for community gardens can enhance corporate social responsibility while meeting state mandates [4](#ref-4), [6](#ref-6).
