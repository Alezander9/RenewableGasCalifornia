# Book and Claim Accounting for RNG

Book and claim accounting is a tracking mechanism that allows for the decoupling of environmental attributes from the physical flow of a commodity, such as Renewable Natural Gas (RNG). In this system, RNG is injected into a common carrier pipeline in one location, and an equivalent amount of gas is "claimed" at another location, often across state lines, without requiring a physical delivery contract between the producer and the end-user [2](#ref-2), [4](#ref-4).

## CARB LCFS Accounting Rules

The California Air Resources Board (CARB) maintains specific requirements for book-and-claim accounting under the [Low Carbon Fuel Standard (LCFS)](lcfs_overview.md), recently updated in the 2024 LCFS Final Regulation Order and Guidance 19-05.

### Core Principles
*   **Pipeline Injection:** To be eligible for book-and-claim accounting, biomethane must be injected into a common carrier pipeline system within North America [4](#ref-4).
*   **Three-Quarter Matching Limit:** Environmental attributes associated with RNG must be matched to physical fuel withdrawal or dispensing within a three-quarter time span. Attributes not claimed within three quarters of injection expire for LCFS purposes [4](#ref-4).
*   **Eligible Uses:** Book-and-claim is permitted for RNG used as Bio-CNG, Bio-LNG, or Bio-L-CNG for transportation; feedstock for renewable hydrogen production; or fuel for electricity generation via fuel cells for EV charging [4](#ref-4).

## 2024 Amendments and Phase-Out Timeline

The 2024 LCFS amendments introduce stricter limits and an eventual phase-out of book and claim accounting for certain uses, reflecting a policy shift toward prioritizing physical deliverability and long-term decarbonization of the gas grid [2](#ref-2).

### Key Restrictions
*   **Phase-Out of RNG Crediting (2040)**: For RNG projects that break ground after December 31, 2029, LCFS credit generation for methane used as a combustion fuel (in vehicles) will be phased out after December 31, 2040 [1](#ref-1).
*   **Physical 'Deliverability' Requirements (50% Flow Rule)**: For projects breaking ground after 2029, reporting entities must demonstrate that the pipeline delivery path physically flows from the injection point toward California at least 50% of the time on an annual basis [2](#ref-2), [4](#ref-4).
*   **Avoided Methane Crediting Phase-Out**: The credit for avoided methane emissions—which often results in negative carbon intensity scores for RNG from dairy and swine manure—will also be phased out by 2040 for projects breaking ground after 2029 [3](#ref-3).
*   **Grandfathering for Existing Projects**: Projects that break ground before January 1, 2030, are "grandfathered" and exempt from the new deliverability requirements. These projects can continue under existing book and claim rules for the duration of their crediting life [3](#ref-3).

### Enforcement Timeline
*   **Bio-CNG/LNG:** Deliverability requirements for new projects (post-2029) begin January 1, 2041 (potentially accelerated to 2037 based on ZEV adoption) [2](#ref-2), [4](#ref-4).
*   **EV Charging/Hydrogen:** Deliverability requirements begin January 1, 2046 [2](#ref-2), [4](#ref-4).

## Impact on the Market

These restrictions are expected to significantly impact out-of-state RNG projects that currently rely on book and claim to access the California LCFS market [2](#ref-2). The "50% flow" rule effectively limits the geographic range of projects that can participate, favoring those closer to California or located on major pipeline corridors with consistent westward flow [3](#ref-3).

### Documentation and Verification
Rigorous documentation is required to prevent double-counting and ensure environmental integrity:
*   **Evidence:** Pathway holders must provide unredacted monthly invoices showing RNG quantities and prices, as well as unredacted contracts for environmental attributes [4](#ref-4).
*   **Attestations:** Upstream parties must provide attestations confirming exclusive rights to the attributes and ensuring no other entity has claimed them in other programs [4](#ref-4).
*   **Verification:** Pathways utilizing book-and-claim biomethane are subject to full verification and cannot defer the process [5](#ref-5).

For a summary of the regulatory risks associated with these changes, see [Regulatory Changes (Book and Claim) - Risk](../risks/regulatory_changes.md).
