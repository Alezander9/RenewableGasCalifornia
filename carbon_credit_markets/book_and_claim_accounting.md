# Book and Claim Accounting - Analysis

Book and claim accounting is a tracking mechanism that allows for the decoupling of environmental attributes from the physical flow of a commodity, such as Renewable Natural Gas (RNG). In this system, RNG is injected into a common carrier pipeline in one location, and an equivalent amount of gas is "claimed" at another location, often across state lines, without requiring a physical delivery contract between the producer and the end-user [2](#ref-2).

## California LCFS Policy Shifts and Debate

The California Air Resources Board (CARB) has recently adopted significant amendments to the Low Carbon Fuel Standard (LCFS) that introduce stricter limits and an eventual phase-out of book and claim accounting for RNG used as a transportation fuel. These changes reflect a policy shift toward prioritizing physical deliverability and long-term decarbonization of the gas grid.

### Key Restrictions and Phase-Out Timeline

1. **Phase-Out of RNG Crediting (2040)**: For RNG projects that break ground after December 31, 2029, LCFS credit generation for methane used as a combustion fuel (in vehicles) will be phased out after December 31, 2040 [1](#ref-1).
2. **New 'Deliverability' Requirements (50% Flow Rule)**: CARB is imposing stricter rules requiring that RNG be delivered via pipelines that physically flow toward California at least 50% of the time on an annual basis [2](#ref-2). This requirement is designed to ensure that the gas being credited actually has a physical path to the California market.
3. **Grandfathering for Existing Projects**: Projects that break ground before January 1, 2030, are "grandfathered" and exempt from the new deliverability requirements. These projects can continue under existing book and claim rules for the duration of their crediting life [3](#ref-3).
4. **Effective Dates**: The new deliverability requirements for new projects (post-2029) are set to take effect on January 1, 2041, for transportation fuel and January 1, 2046, for hydrogen production feedstock [2](#ref-2).
5. **Avoided Methane Crediting Phase-Out**: The credit for avoided methane emissions—which often results in negative carbon intensity scores for RNG from dairy and swine manure—will also be phased out by 2040 for projects breaking ground after 2029 [3](#ref-3).

### Impact on the Market

These restrictions are expected to significantly impact out-of-state RNG projects that currently rely on book and claim to access the California LCFS market [2](#ref-2). The "50% flow" rule effectively limits the geographic range of projects that can participate, favoring those closer to California or located on major pipeline corridors with consistent westward flow [3](#ref-3).
