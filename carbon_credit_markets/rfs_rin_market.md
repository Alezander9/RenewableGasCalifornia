# RFS RIN Market - Analysis

The federal Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) program, administered by the EPA, requires a certain volume of renewable fuel to replace or reduce the quantity of petroleum-based transportation fuel [2](#ref-2). For [Renewable Natural Gas (RNG)](../industry_overview/feedstock_types.md), the program generates Renewable Identification Numbers (RINs) that track compliance with biofuel volume mandates [2](#ref-2). The market for these credits, particularly D3 RINs, has undergone significant shifts due to regulatory changes.

## RIN Categories for RNG

RNG primarily generates two types of RINs based on the feedstock source and the fuel's lifecycle greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions reduction [3](#ref-3):

*   **D3 (Cellulosic Biofuel):** Generated from cellulosic feedstocks such as [landfill gas](../industry_overview/feedstock_types.md), wastewater treatment sludge, and agricultural residues.
*   **D5 (Advanced Biofuel):** Generated from advanced feedstocks that do not meet the cellulosic definition but still achieve at least a 50% GHG reduction.

## RIN Price Volatility and the 'Set Rule'

The EPA's 'Set Rule' for 2023, 2024, and 2025 established new Renewable Volume Obligations (RVO) and introduced a major structural change by eliminating the Cellulosic Waiver Credit (CWC) [4](#ref-4) [5](#ref-5). Previously, the CWC functioned as a de facto price ceiling for D3 RINs. Its removal has allowed D3 RIN prices to be determined by market supply and demand fundamentals rather than an administrative cap [4](#ref-4).

Following the finalization of the Set Rule, D3 RIN prices surged to their highest levels in over a year [4](#ref-4). The market has shown substantial volatility in response to regulatory announcements; for instance, D3 RIN prices jumped by approximately $0.60 following the introduction of a 33% increase in RVO targets [5](#ref-5).

## Impact on Renewable Natural Gas (RNG)

RNG is the primary source for D3 RIN generation [4](#ref-4), making the RNG market highly sensitive to RFS policy shifts.

*   **Supply-Demand Dynamics:** D3 RIN production, largely derived from RNG, has consistently lagged behind EPA mandates. This persistent supply gap, combined with increasing RVO targets, supports elevated RIN prices [6](#ref-6).
*   **Investment and Risk:** Higher D3 RIN prices resulting from the elimination of the CWC provide a stronger financial signal for RNG project development [7](#ref-7). However, the increased price volatility introduces new complexities for project hedging and compliance strategies [7](#ref-7).
*   **Historical Sensitivity:** Market analysts like Rapidan Energy Group have noted that RIN prices (including D4 and D6 categories) are highly sensitive to EPA guidance and RFS volume settings, with historical projections showing significant price spikes during periods of bullish regulatory signals [8](#ref-8).

## RIN Generation Data (2024-2025)

RIN generation for RNG-derived fuels is a significant component of the D3 cellulosic biofuel category. The following table summarizes RIN generation for various RNG-related fuel types in 2024 and 2025.

| Year | RIN Category | Fuel Type | RIN Quantity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2024 | D3 (Cellulosic) | Renewable Compressed Natural Gas | 880,960,893 |
| 2024 | D3 (Cellulosic) | Renewable Liquefied Natural Gas | 82,642,184 |
| 2024 | D3 (Cellulosic) | Renewable Natural Gas - RNG | 9,820,158 |
| 2024 | D5 (Advanced) | Renewable Compressed Natural Gas | 1,252,569 |
| 2025 | D3 (Cellulosic) | Renewable Natural Gas - RNG | 1,096,653,142 |
| 2025 | D3 (Cellulosic) | Renewable Compressed Natural Gas | 1,878,401 |
| 2025 | D3 (Cellulosic) | Renewable Compressed Natural Gas - CNG | 694,019 |
| 2025 | D5 (Advanced) | Renewable Natural Gas - RNG | 387,420 |
| 2025 | D5 (Advanced) | Renewable Compressed Natural Gas - CNG | 547,270 |
| 2025 | D5 (Advanced) | Renewable Compressed Natural Gas | 14,085 |

*Note: The 2025 data reflects the full year as reported in the EPA's December 2025 summary [1](#ref-1).*
