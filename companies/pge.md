# PG&E - RNG and Biomethane Strategy

Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E) is actively developing its Renewable Natural Gas (RNG) infrastructure and procurement strategy to align with California's decarbonization goals.

## Biomethane Strategy
PG&E's biomethane strategy is primarily driven by [California Senate Bill (SB) 1440](../regulations/sb_1440_procurement.md) and CPUC Decision 22-02-025, which mandate specific biomethane procurement targets for non-Natural Gas Vehicle (NGV) bundled core customers [1](#ref-1).

- **Procurement Targets:** The utility aims to procure 7.452 Bcf annually for the short-term (2025-2029) and 27.744 Bcf annually for the medium-term (2030-2040) [1](#ref-1).
- **Feedstock Diversification:** PG&E's strategy focuses on a diverse portfolio that includes anaerobic digestion, pyrolysis, gasification, and methanation of various biomass feedstocks [2](#ref-2).
- **Cost Management:** To protect customers from excessive bill impacts, PG&E employs 'Annual' and 'Program' cost caps while pursuing these environmental targets [1](#ref-1).

## Woody Biomass Pilots
PG&E is exploring the conversion of woody biomass into RNG through research, development, and pilot projects.

- **West Biofuels Project:** In June 2023, PG&E submitted an application for a Woody Biomass to RNG pilot project in partnership with West Biofuels, LLC. The facility, located in Woodland, CA, utilizes gasification and methanation technologies [3](#ref-3).
- **R&D Roadmap:** The utility's R&D plan includes evaluating woody biomass conversion (Field Demo 2022-2025) and woody biomass fermentation (Paper Study 2022-2023) [2](#ref-2).

## RNG Interconnection Standards
Renewable Natural Gas (RNG) interconnection to PG&E's pipeline system is governed by standards that ensure nondiscriminatory access while maintaining safety and gas quality. For broader context, see [Pipeline Interconnection Rules](../regulations/pipeline_interconnection_rules.md).

*   **Interconnection Timeline:** The process typically takes between 18 and 30 months and involves engineering, gas quality testing, and the installation of interconnection skids [4](#ref-4).
*   **AB 1900 (2012):** This foundational legislation required the CPUC to adopt standards for biomethane constituents of concern and pipeline open access rules. [5](#ref-5)
*   **Decision (D.) 14-01-034:** The CPUC decision that formally adopted the health and safety-related standards and protocols for biomethane injection into common carrier pipelines. [7](#ref-7)
*   **Standard Renewable Gas Interconnection Agreement (SRGIA):** Often referred to as the gas equivalent of Electric Rule 21, the SRGIA governs the technical and contractual requirements for connecting RNG facilities to the utility's pipeline system. [8](#ref-8)
*   **Standardization Efforts:** PG&E is developing standardized interconnection skids and gas quality measurement devices to reduce costs and complexity for RNG project developers [2](#ref-2).

## Incentives
Several programs and legislative mandates exist to support RNG production and interconnection in California.

*   **Biomethane Monetary Incentive Program:** Established to encourage in-state production, this program awards 50% of interconnection costs, up to $1.5 million per project, with total program funding of $40 million. [7](#ref-7)
*   **[SB 1383 (2016)](../regulations/sb_1383_methane.md):** Mandates state agencies to adopt policies and incentives to increase sustainable production and use of renewable gas, with a specific focus on dairy methane. [6](#ref-6)
*   **[SB 1440 Procurement Targets](../regulations/sb_1440_procurement.md):** Requires utilities to procure specific amounts of renewable natural gas to meet state climate goals, providing a guaranteed market for producers. [8](#ref-8)

## Barriers
Despite incentives, RNG producers face significant hurdles in connecting to the grid.

*   **Cost Responsibility:** Under D.14-01-034, the costs of complying with health and safety standards and the physical interconnection are primarily borne by the biomethane providers. [7](#ref-7)
*   **Technical Stringency:** Strict testing and monitoring requirements for 'constituents of concern' (as mandated by AB 1900) increase operational complexity and capital expenditure for RNG producers. [5](#ref-5) [8](#ref-8)
*   **Regulatory Complexity:** Overlapping jurisdictions of multiple agencies (CPUC, CARB, CEC) and the need for site-specific environmental reviews can significantly delay project timelines. [8](#ref-8)
