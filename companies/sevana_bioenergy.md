# Sevana Bioenergy - Overview

Sevana Bioenergy, founded in 2017 and headquartered in Boise, Idaho, is a developer and operator of large-scale renewable natural gas (RNG) projects in North America [3](#ref-3) [7](#ref-7). The company is led by CEO Steve Compton [8](#ref-8).

## Assets and Projects

### Rialto Bioenergy Facility (RBF)
In June 2024, Sevana Bioenergy acquired the Rialto Bioenergy Facility for $20 million at auction following the bankruptcy of a subsidiary of [Anaergia](anaergia.md) [1](#ref-1) [2](#ref-2) [3](#ref-3). Located in Rialto, California, the facility is the largest landfill-diverted organic waste-to-RNG facility in North America, with a processing capacity of 1,000 tons per day (700 tons of food waste and 300 tons of municipal biosolids) [1](#ref-1) [3](#ref-3). In November 2024, Sevana's affiliate, Rialto Bioenergy Solutions LLC, entered into a 10-year operations and maintenance (O&M) contract with Anaergia Services LLC to manage the facility [4](#ref-4).

### MBL Bioenergy
Sevana is a partner in MBL Bioenergy, a joint venture with UGI Energy Services and [California Bioenergy (CalBio)](calbio.md). The partnership develops RNG projects on dairy farms in South Dakota. An initial $70 million investment was committed for a cluster of farms expected to produce 300 million cubic feet of RNG annually by 2024 [6](#ref-6).

### Idaho RNG Project
In partnership with Meridiam, Sevana Bioenergy developed a biogas-to-RNG project in Idaho. The project involved upgrading an existing facility to displace approximately 2 million gallons of diesel per year [5](#ref-5).

## Investment and Partnerships

### Ontario Teachers’ Pension Plan (OTPP)
In 2023, the Ontario Teachers’ Pension Plan acquired a majority stake in Sevana Bioenergy. OTPP committed $250 million in capital to finance the development of RNG projects across North America [7](#ref-7).

### Regenerative Agriculture
Sevana Bioenergy was featured in a Newsweek report on regenerative agriculture, highlighting its role in transforming agricultural byproducts into renewable energy [8](#ref-8).
